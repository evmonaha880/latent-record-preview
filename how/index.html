How it was made

Why I wrote it

I'm from Rochester, New York. George Eastman and the company he built changed the world, and they shaped where I'm from. I feel compelled to tell his whole story, because I'm afraid he doesn't get the recognition he deserves outside Rochester.

Evan chose the subject, the argument and every decision. Claude researched, drafted and checked. This is the method, in the order it happened.

Two Claude models did the writing: Claude Opus 5 from 3 to 19 August, then Claude Fable 5 from 19 to 21 August. A third, Claude Sonnet 5, ran one review on 4 August and changed nothing.

April 2025

It began with two nephews and a hard question

Evan was explaining how film works to his two nine-year-old nephews and could not find the words. So he used an AI, out loud, by voice, to explain the chemistry of film. That made him wonder how he could tell this whole story with AI.

The chemistry also gave the project its frame. A latent image is the picture that is already on the film but cannot be seen until it is developed.

March 2026

A tour, and a feeling that pieces were missing

Evan emailed a curator at the George Eastman Museum and got a private tour. He left inspired and overwhelmed by the scale of the history, and sure the story was missing big pieces and needed to be told. The curator sent him the 1920 interview with Eastman that same day.

Through summer 2026

Before the first draft, and all through it, Claude read and indexed the material: two full biographies, the MIT letters, declassified spy-satellite records, a book on the Kodak and Polaroid war. Evan chose what went in. The sources page lists each one and what it gave the essay.

3 August

Ten ideas, and none of them won

Evan and Claude laid out ten candidate theses and ran all ten through /story, a storytelling coach Evan set up for Claude. It checks a draft beat by beat against a library of storytelling craft, suggests moves, and only rewrites when Evan says go. He ran it again on the argument on 4 August and on a full read-through on 15 August.

None of the ten won. At 7:36 pm Evan wrote the line that did: what is feared more is him being completely forgotten about to history. The thesis page shows all ten, and the one that landed.

3 to 21 August

He wrote in the margins; Claude rewrote

Most of the sentences are Claude's. Evan steered. He left notes in the margin saying what was wrong, struck out passages himself, sent chat messages with new ideas, and sometimes rewrote a line by hand. Claude produced the next draft. He read it and sent it round again. The grid beside every paragraph in the essay is the record of those rounds.

Throughout

Claude checked, and was caught checking

20 to 21 August

The first reader, and one last fix

The essay went to its first reader on 20 August. Hours later, Claude looked at the photograph of Eastman's last note and found the essay said "seven words" when the note is three short lines. The fix was the last edit the essay ever got. The text has not changed since 21 August.

Reading the grids

It works like Wordle. Each row of squares is one draft of a paragraph, and each square is one sentence. Green means the sentence was kept as it was. Gold means it was reworded. Dark means it was cut. A red dot means a note of his changed the text. A blue dot means a chat message of his did.

The editor saved every few seconds and did not record who typed a given word, so the page never claims who wrote a particular word. It shows the note that came before each change, so you can see what was asked for and what came back.

What the record does not cover

  • Before 3 August. Fifteen months of research came first, much of it in other chat tools. Only pieces survive.
  • Two outside research runs. A reading of the MIT plaque (later found wrong) and a long research run on 19 August came from another tool and are not in the record.
  • Reading and thinking. What Claude read is logged with times. What Evan read on paper, and what he thought, are not.
  • Early readers. Their comments came back outside the record.
  • A few lost backups. Some backups from 4 August were deleted before the record was built.

The record was rebuilt from the raw save files, not from anyone's summary, and checked against them by program. Nothing touched the essay after its last save on 21 August.
