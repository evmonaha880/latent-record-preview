How it was made

This site shows one essay, Forgetting the Man Who Manufactured Memory, as it was made: 568 saves over three weeks in August 2026. Open any paragraph to see how it changed, and what the author wrote in the margin before each change.

Who did what

Evan Monahan, the author

Chose the subject and the argument. Left notes in the margin saying what to change, wrote some passages himself, pasted in passages drafted in chat, and cut what did not belong.

Claude

Drafted and revised the text in Claude Code, working in the same file. Two models did the writing:

  • Claude Opus 5 · 3 August to 19 August · 943 replies
  • Claude Fable 5 · 19 August to 21 August · 607 replies

One review ran on Claude Sonnet 5 on 4 August. It read the draft and changed nothing.

The three weeks

  1. 3 Aug1 save The first save, in the evening. The outline becomes an essay.
  2. 4 Aug191 saves The first long day of revision.
  3. 5–16 Augno saves Twelve days with no saves to the essay. But the work went on: on 5 August Claude marks the plan ratified; on 7 August the essay is copied to the author’s laptop; on 15 August the author asks Claude for a review of the essay’s story.
  4. 17 Aug4 saves Work resumes. No saves on the 18th.
  5. 19 Aug116 saves The model changes from Claude Opus 5 to Claude Fable 5.
  6. 20 Aug255 saves Passages drafted in chat are pasted in.
  7. 21 Aug1 save The last save. The text has not changed since.

Reading the drafts

It works like Wordle. Each row of squares is one draft of a paragraph, and each square is one sentence. Green means the sentence was kept as it was. Gold means it was reworded. Dark means it was cut.

A red dot means a note of his changed the text. A blue dot means a chat message of his did. The editor saved every few seconds and did not record who typed a particular word, so the page never says who wrote a given word. It shows the note that came before each change, so you can see what was asked for and what came back.

How we know the record is right

The record was rebuilt from the raw save files, not from anyone’s summary, and a program checked it against those files. Each save matches the file it came from. Every note, sentence and paragraph lines up with the published essay. Nothing touched the essay after its last save on 21 August.

What the record does not cover

Before 3 August

Fifteen months of research came before the essay, much of it in other chat tools. Only pieces survive; The thesis shows the ideas that came first.

Two outside research runs

Two inputs came from another tool and are not in the record: a reading of the MIT plaque from a photograph on 4 August, later found wrong, and a long research run on 19 August.

Reading and thinking

What Claude read, and what the author handed it, are logged with times. What he read on paper, and what he thought, are not.

Early readers’ comments

Copies went to a few early readers as shared documents. Their comments came back outside the record.

A few lost backups

Some backups from 4 August were deleted before the record was built. One save from that day survives as text only.
